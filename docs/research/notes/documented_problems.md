Evidence tiers: A = government/court/official vendor notice; B = news report; C = vendor/consultant marketing; D = forum (no forum posts were retrieved, so tier D is empty).

## Which categories have documented, credible evidence?

### Takeaway
Strong evidence exists for (1) the Dec 2021 outage and its wage litigation, and (2) 2025-26 county audits where UKG overtime calculation did not match collective-bargaining rules (Manatee, Volusia). Older municipal audits show control weaknesses around Kronos. Evidence is thin or absent for security/access misconfiguration, release regressions (one vendor-disclosed DST bug found), and WFC-to-Pro WFM migration data quality.

### Cited Findings

#### A. Configuration inconsistencies / pay rule errors (documented, tier A/B)
- Manatee County, FL EMS: audit (reported June 2026) found EMS workers underpaid by at least $336,607 over nine months because the system did not correctly count hours worked toward overtime as required by the collective bargaining agreement; reporting ties it to new overtime rules after a June 2025 bargaining agreement; county's Kronos contract dates from 2021 under the former company name. Root cause per reporting: pay rule not matching CBA. Classification: pay rule/configuration vs contract change. Credibility: audit via news, good, primary audit not read. — [WUSF](https://www.wusf.org/politics-issues/2026-06-27/manatee-county-underpaid-ems-workers-by-at-least-336-607-audit-finds); [EMS1](https://www.ems1.com/labor-issues/audit-finds-fla-county-underpaid-ems-personnel-more-than-336k)
- Volusia County, FL EMS: audit released "in March" (year not confirmed in the summary) reported UKG payroll errors with some workers underpaid by up to $2,000 and others overpaid by more than $10,000; a separate report says nearly 30 EMTs faced repayment demands of $76 to $62,000 after a payroll coding error, with the union pushing back. Exact audit date and totals not established. — [EMS1 repayment story](https://www.ems1.com/labor-issues/fla-emts-told-to-repay-overpayments-after-payroll-error); [Fox 35](https://www.fox35orlando.com/video/fmc-e4wpfv01ce6z3u2m); Volusia internal audit reports exist at [volusia.org](https://www.volusia.org/core/fileparse.php/7023/urlt/2023-05_Audit_Report_Payroll_ADA.pdf) (2023-05 payroll audit; contents not read, may be unrelated to the EMS issue).
- Fayetteville, NC: audits found Kronos rules set from verbal guidance/past practice rather than written policy; a police review traced payroll errors to hours processed in the wrong 28-day FLSA period. Dates not established (document IDs only). Tier A, summary-level. — [Fayetteville audit 1](https://fayettevillenc.gov/home/showpublisheddocument/21178/637922792448230000); [audit 2](https://fayettevillenc.gov/home/showpublisheddocument/26261/638622626282600000)
- Dallas, 2015: audit of civilian timekeeping found Lawson and Kronos lacked input controls to prevent paid-leave errors. Tier A. — [Dallas City Auditor A15-011](https://dallascityhall.com/departments/auditor/DCH%20Documents/Audit%20Reports%20FY%202015%20(3-20-2015%20to%2009-30-2015)/A15-011%20-%20Audit%20of%20City%20of%20Dallas'%20Civilian%20Timekeeping%20Internal%20Controls%20and%20Processes%2008-14-2015.pdf)
- Oklahoma City 2023: public works audit found manual acting-pay-rate updates error-prone. Tier A, summary-level. — [OKC audit](https://okc.gov/home/showpublisheddocument/35758/638228548416070000)
- Australia, ALDI: class action over a Kronos clock-in system that rounded punches up to 10 minutes before/after rostered times so extra time went unpaid; about A$55M settlement for 32,308 current/former staff, Federal Court approval reported as 23 Oct 2026; ALDI denied liability. Classification: rounding rule configuration (alleged, not found by a court). Only one secondary source; the search summary's "Oct 2026" date should be verified. — [HC Online](https://www.hcamag.com/au/specialisation/employment-law/aldis-55m-class-action-settles-unpaid-shift-work-for-32000-staff/594780)
- Search found no US Kronos rounding-rule case in this pass (the search summary said so); that is a search gap, not proof none exists.

Attacker attribution not found in my sources. — [AHA H-ISAC](https://www.aha.org/h-isac-reports/2021-12-13-h-isac-tlp-white-kronos-private-cloud-kpc-ransomware-incident-causes); [BleepingComputer](https://www.bleepingcomputer.com/news/security/kronos-ransomware-attack-may-cause-weeks-of-hr-solutions-downtime/amp/); [SHRM](https://www.shrm.org/topics-tools/news/technology/ukg-hack-disrupts-scheduling-payroll-thousands-employers)

### Inferences
- The most defensible "configuration" cases are audits where pay rules diverged from a collective bargaining agreement (Manatee, Volusia): a change in the contract was not matched by a correct rule configuration. This fits a change-impact tool, but the causal chain comes from news summaries, so confirm in the audit PDFs.
- The 2021 outage is evidence of vendor dependency and absent fallback processes, not of configuration error; classify as availability. Its payroll damage (estimated hours, missing overtime) came from the employers' manual workarounds.
- Municipal audits mostly show control and documentation gaps (verbal rules, missing approvals, manual rate updates) rather than traceable configuration defects.
